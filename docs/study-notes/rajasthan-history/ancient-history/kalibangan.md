## Claims deliberately qualified or not included

- **Name meaning “black bangles”:** this is included with attribution to B. K. Thapar's UNESCO-hosted chapter, which links the name to weather-stained terracotta bangle fragments. ASI Part II Chapter 15 (Madhu Bala) independently mentions thousands of scattered bangles but does not specify their colour. Keep the two descriptions distinct.
- **“World's earliest ploughed field”:** not stated as an unqualified fact. The Gazetteer itself uses “perhaps”; NCERT confirms a ploughed field at Kalibangan and the cross-furrow inference.
- **Earthquake around 2700 BCE:** only a possible explanation for abandonment; the Gazetteer says it is not conclusively proven.
- **First discoverer:** do not collapse James Tod's recorded mention, Tessitori's visits, Aurel Stein's survey, Ghosh's 1952 highlighting, and the 1961–69 ASI excavation into one “discoverer” claim.
- **Coins:** excluded from Q&A for now. A tourism summary lists coins, but the precise object identity and context need verification against ASI excavation records; do not conflate a coin-like object with evidence of coin currency.
- **Fire altars and ritual:** the Gazetteer records seven fire altars on a platform and says some platforms seem ritual in use. Do not claim the exact ritual or deity without independent archaeological support.

## Source disagreements and editorial corrections

- **Cemetery mound code:** B. B. Lal's 1979 Fig. 1 labels the cemetery KLB-4 (S16, printed p. 66), whereas the Hanumangarh District Gazetteer (S4, p. 36) and ASI Jodhpur Circle museum page (S7, “Harappan Site Kalibangan”) use KLB-8. Keep both labels attributed and do not silently harmonize them until the original excavation plan/object register is checked.
- **Chronology ranges:** the Hanumangarh Gazetteer (S4, p. 36) gives approximate ranges for Early and Mature Harappan phases. B. B. Lal's 1979 synthesis (S16, p. 94) separately tabulates uncorrected radiocarbon values (Period I 2400–2250 BCE; Period II 2200–1700 BCE) and then MASCA/Clark-corrected values (Period I 2900–2700 BCE; Period II 2500–2000 BCE). These are source- and correction-method-specific historical estimates, not one reconciled modern chronology. Do not choose one without specialist review of the samples and correction method.


- **Rice remains at Kalibangan:** ASI Jodhpur Circle's museum page mentions rice among crops, but the ASI *Indian Archaeology 1974–75 — A Review* (printed p. 79) records Vishnu-Mittre and R. Savithri's re-examination: plant remains from terracotta cakes and pai were identified as barley and, in a few cases, wheat; absence of rice epidermis and its characteristic chess-board pattern led them to reject the earlier rice identification as not soundly based. Keep the site-specific crop claim disputed; do not state rice as a confirmed Kalibangan crop without resolving the primary archaeobotanical evidence.



- **Crop-name clarification:** ASI Part II Chapter 14 prints “Cicer arietinum (Chana, black gram)”. The botanical name conflicts with its parenthetical “black gram” gloss: the Government of India's PPV&FRA lists Cicer arietinum as chickpea/chana and Vigna mungo as black gram. The note therefore uses the botanical name and does not repeat “black gram” as its answer. A reviewer should check the original archaeobotanical identification.
- **Rice / horse gram scope:** the ASI Jodhpur Circle museum webpage lists rice and horse gram among crops at Kalibangan, but the ASI Part II Chapter 14 passage discusses wheat, barley, pea and Cicer arietinum; its nearby footnote refers to rice and horse gram evidence from Kunal. Keep rice/horse gram as an unresolved site-specific source discrepancy until the excavation sample record is reviewed.
